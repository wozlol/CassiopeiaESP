START HERE

This card holds reference material for when you cannot reach a doctor, a pharmacist or the internet. The material comes from public sources such as the NHS medicines pages and WikiMed. It is reference, not medical advice, and it can be out of date or written for another country. When you can reach real help, use it.

HOW TO SEARCH

Library, then "Search everything". Type what you notice or the medicine name, then press Enter. Everyday words work: stomach ache, burning pee, snake bite, tylenol. Results show where each one comes from at the right edge. Enter reads the article. Tab moves between links inside an article, Enter follows a link, Backspace goes back. Slash searches inside the page.

Open one pack instead of searching everything to look up titles by their first letters. Tab there switches to word search for that pack only.

A FEW THINGS THAT ARE WIDELY TAUGHT

Not responding and not breathing normally: get emergency help if there is any way to, then push hard and fast on the centre of the chest, about 100 to 120 times a minute, about 5 to 6 cm (2 inches) deep for an adult.

Severe bleeding: press firmly on the wound with a cloth and keep pressing. A tourniquet placed above the wound on an arm or leg is for bleeding that is life threatening and does not stop with pressure.

Severe allergic reaction with swelling of the face or throat, trouble breathing or fainting: use an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto injector in the outer thigh if one is available, and get emergency help.

Choking and unable to cough or speak: alternate back blows and abdominal thrusts.

Several handbooks (for example the Army and Special Forces medical books) are scanned pages read by a computer. The text can contain mistakes, especially in numbers, doses and the degree sign, a temperature may come out as 1030 instead of 103 degrees. Check any dose against the medicine's own label or a pharmacist before you use it.

Before taking any medicine, check what is already being taken, allergies, pregnancy, age and the strength printed on the pack. Do not give aspirin to children. Doses in the packs can differ from the pack in your hand, trust the pack and a pharmacist over the pack here.

This is a reminder and not training. A first aid course is worth far more than this page.
